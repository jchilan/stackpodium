Pick n8n if the agent has to act inside your tools and you want a source-available base you can self-host. Pick Make if you want the easiest visual builder for a first agent and you do not need to self-host.

Questions

Which should I pick?

For an agent that has to act inside your tools, n8n is #1: source-available, with native AI nodes, and you can self-host. Make is #2, and it is the easier visual start for a first agent. Zapier, not in this pair, is #3 when you want the widest catalogue. If a visual flow is not enough, the next ranking is a custom agent in code.

What do the plans cost?

Checked November 2026. n8n's pricing page: self-host the Community edition, or cloud Starter at €20/mo, Pro at €50/mo, and Business at €667/mo, each billed annually. Enterprise is contact sales. The Business card is marked self-hosted. One execution is a full workflow run, not a single step. n8n's help center says Cloud Starter includes 2,500 executions a month, and it names Pro-1 at 10,000 and Pro-2 at 50,000, without tying those names to the €50 price on the pricing card. Make's comparison page: Free is $0 for 1,000 credits a month, then Core from $9/mo, Pro from $16/mo, and Team from $29/mo, starting at 10,000 credits. Most actions use 1 credit. Make says annual billing costs less than monthly. We are not quoting a separate month-to-month slider price, because that figure was not in the page we could read.

What are the main limits?

n8n's help center says a Cloud run that would pass the monthly execution quota fails instead of queueing. The Community edition is under the Sustainable Use License: your own internal, personal, or non-commercial use. n8n does not call that license open source. Make's pricing page says scenarios stop when credits run out, and that routers and error handlers do not use credits. Its pricing FAQ says there are more than 1,000 standard apps. We did not confirm a Make edition you can self-host.
